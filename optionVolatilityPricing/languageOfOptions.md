## 📚 Topics Covered

### 1. The Language of Options
- Options markets bring together traders with different goals: speculation, hedging, arbitrage, or market-making.
- Every trader must understand the terminology and rules to communicate effectively and manage risk.
- Key distinction: **Options give rights, not obligations**, unlike futures contracts.

---

### 2. Contract Specifications
- **Call Option:** Right to buy (long position) at a fixed price before expiration.  
- **Put Option:** Right to sell (short position) at a fixed price before expiration.  
- **Underlying Asset:** The security, commodity, index, or futures contract tied to the option.  
- **Strike Price:** The fixed price at which the underlying can be bought or sold.  
- **Expiration Date:** The last day the option can be exercised.  
- Exchange-traded options have standardized terms; dealer-issued options can be customized.  
- Example: A crude oil October 21 call gives the right to buy one October crude oil futures contract (1,000 barrels) at $21 per barrel before expiration.

---

### 3. Exercise and Assignment
- **Exercise:** The option holder chooses to convert the option into a position in the underlying.  
  - Call → Long position in underlying.  
  - Put → Short position in underlying.  
- **Assignment:** The seller of the option is obligated to take the opposite side when the buyer exercises.  
- **American Options:** Can be exercised anytime before expiration.  
- **European Options:** Can only be exercised on expiration day.  
- U.S. exchange-traded stock and futures options are typically American.

---

### 4. Market Integrity
- Market integrity is guaranteed through a chain of responsibility:
  1. **Individual Trader** must fulfill obligations.  
  2. **Clearing Firm** backs the trader’s obligations.  
  3. **Clearing House** guarantees all trades, acting as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer.  
- This system ensures no trader faces default risk when exercising an option.

---

### 5. Margin Requirements
- Traders must deposit margin (good faith capital) to initiate trades.  
- Margin ensures traders can meet obligations if markets move against them.  
- **Margin Call:** Ensures future obligations can be met.  
- **Variation Call:** Covers immediate realized losses.  
- Margin requirements vary by position type and risk exposure.  
- Professional traders may receive reduced margin requirements.

---

### 6. Settlement Procedures
- **Stock-Type Settlement:** Requires full payment upfront; profits/losses are unrealized until liquidation.  
- **Futures-Type Settlement:** Requires margin only; profits/losses are realized daily (marked-to-market).  
- U.S. stock options → settled like stock.  
- U.S. futures options → underlying settled like futures, but options settled like stock (can cause cash flow issues).  
- Many non-U.S. exchanges simplify by aligning option and underlying settlement methods.
